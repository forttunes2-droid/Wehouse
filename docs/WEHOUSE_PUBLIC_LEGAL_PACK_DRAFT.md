## 1. Platform Terms

WeHouse provides technology for discovering housing, making accommodation reservations, finding roommates, requesting independent services, communicating about those relationships, and operating participating properties and hotels. WeHouse is not automatically the landlord, hotel, employer, recruiter, agent, or service provider in every transaction. The booking screen must identify the contracting supplier and WeHouse’s role for that product.

Accounts are for people aged **18 or older**. Users must provide accurate information, protect access codes and credentials, use only payment routes shown by WeHouse, and avoid fraud, harassment, discrimination, illegal content, impersonation, unsafe work, or attempts to move protected transactions off-platform.

One person has one Personal identity. A Worker, Property Partner, Hotel or team workspace adds capabilities; it does not replace that identity. Workspace access may be limited, suspended or removed for safety, authority, legal or security reasons.

Listings, availability, identity checks and badges reduce risk but are not guarantees. Users must read the specific price, dates, inclusions, cancellation terms, safety information and supplier identity before confirming.

## 2. Age and eligibility

WeHouse requires account holders to be 18 or older. Date of birth is private and used to confirm eligibility and meet legal/safety obligations. It is not displayed on public profiles. WeHouse may request further evidence where required. False eligibility information may lead to restriction or closure, subject to required record retention and dispute rights.

## 3. Housing and Short Let terms

### Long Let

The customer chooses the tenure, reviews the contract total, completes the required payment and Payment Protection steps, and proceeds to tenancy handover. There is **no Long Let security deposit** in the WeHouse product. The paid tenure and contract total must not be changed without a controlled contract and payment process.

### Short Let

The customer chooses available dates and guests, reviews the total price and cancellation rule, pays, and receives a confirmed stay. Authorised Property Operations verifies the booking code at arrival. Short Let uses check-in and checkout language; it is never described as move-in.

A Short Let public page may show the property’s public street/area before booking. Exact entrance instructions, unit/door details, access codes, host contact details and precise directions are released only to an eligible confirmed guest at the appropriate time.

## 4. Hotel booking terms

The customer selects a room type, package, dates and guest count and sees the total before payment. A temporary hold is not a confirmed booking. Only verified payment confirms the stay. Hotel check-in opens at the hotel’s published local time and requires a ready room and authorised hotel staff. Checkout records departure and moves the assigned room into its operational cleaning state.

The hotel conversation opens only for a paid eligible stay and is for arrival, room and stay arrangements. Payment, refund, cancellation, safety and formal disputes remain handled through the booking and WeHouse support record.

## 5. Worker marketplace and trust

Worker onboarding and ordinary use are free. Workers act as independent service providers unless a separate written arrangement states otherwise. Before launch, counsel must confirm whether WeHouse’s actual activities require a recruiter/private employment agency licence or another labour authorisation.

“Reviewed” means WeHouse completed the published review process. “Trusted” is a higher earned status under published performance and safety rules. Customer ratings come only from completed, paid WeHouse jobs. None of these is a guarantee of future work.

An optional paid Worker subscription buys only the tools and placement benefits shown on its purchase screen. It does **not** buy Reviewed, Trusted, a rating, skill, safety or endorsement. A Worker without the subscription remains discoverable, bookable and able to complete the normal job flow.

For each service request, the Worker and customer agree the work, full price and date. The customer pays through WeHouse. The Worker marks when work starts and when it is ready for review. The customer confirms only after checking the work, or opens a dispute. Payment release, refunds and disputes follow the disclosed booking policy.

## 6. Paid professional plan

The purchase screen must show the plan name, included features, monthly and yearly prices, billing channel, renewal timing, cancellation route and effect of expiry before purchase. Prices are controlled through the authorised Creator configuration and the applicable app-store product configuration; published code should not be edited merely to change a normal plan price.

Web subscriptions use the disclosed web payment provider. Native-app digital subscriptions use the applicable native store where store rules require it. One server entitlement is authoritative. A second checkout is blocked while an active or pending entitlement exists. Cancellation stops future renewal but keeps benefits until the paid period ends. Failed renewal does not create a new trust state; benefits end after any disclosed grace period. No charge changes Reviewed, Trusted or customer-rating status.

Initial benefits approved for launch are professional work insights based only on real WeHouse records, quotes and invoices, configurable record retention, priority support service levels, and clearly labelled Sponsored placement where separately eligible. Insights must never invent demand, earnings, conversion or performance data.

## 7. Payments, cancellations, refunds and disputes

The checkout screen must identify the total amount, purpose, recipient/supplier, applicable fee and refund rule. A browser redirect or screenshot is not payment confirmation; only server verification and the matching Payment Protection record are authoritative. Duplicate and conflicting confirmations go to review rather than creating a second entitlement or release.

Users may cancel where the booking screen permits. A paid booking cannot be silently cancelled: the applicable refund calculation and any provider/consumed-service deductions must be shown and recorded. WeHouse will not use a blanket “no refunds” term. Non-delivery, material misdescription, duplicate charge, unauthorised payment, unsafe arrival, provider cancellation and legal rights remain reviewable.

Formal disputes create a case linked to the booking and preserve relevant messages, payment records and evidence. Money subject to an eligible dispute is frozen or controlled according to the published protection policy. Parties receive the outcome and available escalation route.

## 8. Messages, content and safety

Conversations remain linked to their purpose: roommate match, Worker job, hotel stay, reservation/property operation or WeHouse case. Drafting text does not create a conversation or notification; the first successful send creates the eligible record and message. Users may remove a conversation from their own view, but financial, safety, legal and dispute records may be retained.

Users must have rights to upload text, photos, video, GIFs, stickers, voice notes or documents. WeHouse may restrict or remove unlawful or unsafe content and may preserve evidence required for safety, complaints or law. Emergency situations should be reported to the appropriate emergency authority; WeHouse support is not an emergency service.

## 9. Privacy Notice

WeHouse processes account and contact details, private age eligibility, profile and workspace details, approximate and precise location where provided, listing/property evidence, booking and payment records, messages and attachments, device/security records, support cases, reviews and app-use events.

Purposes include providing requested services, verifying identity/eligibility/authority, protecting payments and safety, preventing fraud, operating support, meeting legal obligations, improving reliability and sending permitted communications. The final notice must list the lawful basis used for each purpose.

Data may be shared only as necessary with the relevant customer, Worker, property/hotel operator, authorised WeHouse staff, payment and cloud providers, verification providers, professional advisers and public authorities where lawfully required. The final notice must identify processors, international transfers and safeguards.

WeHouse applies access controls, encryption where designed, audit logs and retention rules. No system is risk-free. Users may request access, correction, deletion where applicable, restriction, objection, portability and information about automated decisions through [PRIVACY EMAIL]. WeHouse will explain any lawful reason a financial, safety or legal record cannot yet be deleted.

Retention periods must be published by record class rather than promised as “forever.” Age eligibility, identity evidence, payments, contracts, disputes, security logs, chats and marketing preferences require separate schedules. A data incident process, DPIA, processor agreements and data-subject request process must exist before launch.

## 10. Cookies and communications

Strictly necessary storage may be used for authentication, security, checkout continuity and preferences. Analytics, advertising or non-essential tracking must not run until the required notice and consent choice is available. Marketing messages require the applicable permission and an unsubscribe route. Transactional and safety messages may still be sent when necessary to provide the service or protect an account.

## 11. Complaints and changes

Complaints should first be sent to [SUPPORT EMAIL] with the booking/reference and relevant evidence. The final policy must state response targets, escalation, governing law, courts or agreed dispute forum without removing mandatory consumer rights. Material changes require a new version, effective date and renewed acceptance where legally required.
